# 1.7.1 — Surveillance viewport and WebRTC startup diagnostics

Status: **release-ready / production canary required**  
Release line: **1.7.1**

## Production observations from 1.7.0

The first 1.7.0 canary confirmed that ordinary cameras were selecting the
WebRTC path, but exposed two defects:

1. the previous `max-height: 52dvh` rule still allowed portrait/tall parking
   video to consume too much of the Telegram viewport;
2. the Mini App showed `WebRTC` as soon as a remote track was announced,
   even when the player remained at `0:00` and no decoded frame appeared.

Observed examples:

- `Двор_1`: WebRTC selected but no visible frame after more than 30 seconds;
- a parking camera: visible video only after more than 20 seconds.

A remote WebRTC track is not proof of successful media delivery/rendering.

## Compact surveillance viewport

Ordinary surveillance video now uses a dedicated compact display height:

`min(34dvh, 320px)`

with `object-fit: contain`.

The Comelit entrance viewer keeps its previous, larger intercom-specific bound.

## WebRTC negotiation alignment

The Mini App signaling path is aligned more closely with Home Assistant's native
WebRTC frontend:

- offer receive direction includes both audio and video;
- audio and video recv-only transceivers are created;
- local ICE candidates are buffered until the server confirms that the Home
  Assistant provider has registered the WebRTC session;
- remote candidates without an explicit media id receive the same defensive
  `sdpMid=0` normalization used by the HA frontend;
- failed ICE continues to use the existing HLS fallback.

The Mini App remains muted and only attaches the remote video track to the
viewer.

## First-frame diagnostics

Startup status now distinguishes:

```text
WebRTC · подключение…
WebRTC · ICE <state>
WebRTC · track получен, ждём кадр…
WebRTC · ожидание кадра <N> с · RTP <bytes> · decoded <frames>
WebRTC · первый кадр <N.N> с
```

The successful WebRTC label is tied to the first decoded/rendered video frame,
not merely `ontrack`.

While waiting, browser WebRTC statistics report inbound video RTP bytes and
decoded frames. This provides a production-safe way to distinguish:

- signaling/ICE problems;
- no incoming RTP from the source;
- RTP arriving but no decoded frames;
- slow first keyframe/source startup;
- successful first-frame rendering.

No source URL, credentials, SDP, ICE credentials, or camera secret is exposed
in this diagnostic label.

## Unchanged

- no camera preload;
- no Frigate dependency;
- HLS remains the ordinary-camera fallback;
- the Comelit entrance camera stays on its validated media-manager/HLS path;
- Door/Gate behavior is unchanged;
- one-shot/no-retry Door safety is unchanged.

## Production canary

After updating to 1.7.1 and restarting Home Assistant:

1. open `Двор_1`;
2. wait until either a first frame appears or at least 15 seconds have elapsed;
3. record the complete status line shown below the video;
4. repeat for `Паркинг 6048`;
5. compare one of the same cameras in Home Assistant's native live camera UI.

Interpretation:

- `RTP 0 B` after several seconds points to ICE/source delivery startup;
- RTP bytes increasing with `decoded 0` points to codec/keyframe/decode startup;
- decoded frames increasing but no visible frame points to browser/render logic;
- native HA fast + Mini App slow points to the Mini App signaling path;
- native HA equally slow points upstream toward the camera/RTSP/go2rtc source
  lifecycle rather than HLS segmentation.

Door/Gate testing is outside this canary.
